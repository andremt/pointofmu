Where the Assumption family asks you to judge an argument, the Structure family asks you to **describe** it, no evaluating whether the reasoning is any good, just accurately reporting what it's doing.

## Identify the conclusion

Find the main conclusion. The two standard traps are the intermediate conclusion (a claim that itself gets used as a premise for something bigger) and the opposing view the author sets up specifically to argue against.

A useful check: the **"therefore" test.** Take two candidate sentences, put one before an imagined "therefore" and the other after. Whichever direction actually reads correctly identifies the main conclusion, because a real premise-to-conclusion relationship only makes sense read one way.

Conclusions are frequently **not** the last sentence of the stimulus. Missing this because you defaulted to "last sentence = conclusion" isn't a careless slip, it's a specific, nameable gap (the tracker calls it `core-id`) worth tracking as its own pattern rather than writing off as an isolated mistake.

## Argument structure

*"the claim that X plays which one of the following roles?" / "the argument proceeds by..."*

Identify the function a specific claim plays in the argument, or describe the argument's overall method. Answer choices here are deliberately abstract, "a specific case offered in support of a general claim," that kind of language. The fix is to substitute the argument's actual content back into the abstract wording and check whether the resulting sentence is a true description of what's actually happening in the stimulus.

## Point at issue

Two speakers, and the question asks what they actually disagree about (or, less commonly, agree about). The credited answer has to be something **both** speakers took a real position on, and took *opposite* positions on. The most common trap is an answer only one speaker actually addressed; if speaker two never said anything about the topic in the answer choice, it can't be the point at issue between them, no matter how plausible it sounds as a disagreement they *might* have.
